# Modes

A mode is a look on top of the light and dark theme. **Classic** is the panel
as it always was and is always there. Other modes start sealed and open as you
use the panel. How each one opens is left out of these docs and the changelog
on purpose: finding it is part of the fun.

## The Modes page

The palette icon at the bottom of the menu opens it (`/modes`), as does Modes
in Ctrl+K. Both appear once you find your first mode, by one of its ways in or
its word; a mode opened from Developer options does not show them. The mode you use is the wide card on top, with:

- a small picture of the panel in that mode,
- the theme switch (Light, Dark, System), the same as in the top bar,
- its effects, each a switch of its own (Classic has none),
- **Own names**, on unless you switch it off: the mode's names for pages and
  its few lines of its own. Off keeps the panel's usual names and texts, with
  the mode's look and effects as they are. It is kept per mode and stays on
  with reduced motion, since nothing moves.

Below are the other modes, and under them the **Bonus** modes. An unlocked one
has **Activate** and **Preview**, which shows it on the Modes page for 20
seconds without saving it. A mode you use for the first time comes with its
effects on. A sealed one is blurred behind a lock and shows a riddle for each
way it opens; once you are past half way on one of them, its riddle says **The
seal is weakening**.

## Bonus modes

The Bonus section holds modes that open by collecting rather than by a riddle:
each opens once you have found enough of the other modes, by their ways in or
their words (a mode opened from Developer options does not count). Until then
its card is a question mark with a dash for each mode it needs, filled as you
find them. When one opens, it does not interrupt you: the palette icon in the
menu pulses, and its dialog waits for you on the Modes page. Modes
found before a bonus mode existed count too.

## A line for today

The first time you open the panel each day, a short line shows in a small
dialog: a thought, a bit of wisdom or a fact, set as a quote with where it
comes from. One word in it is in bold. The quote icon in the top bar opens it
again for the rest of the day; the panel remembers it showed it, so another
browser does not show it again that day.

## When a mode opens

The panel shows it once, in a dialog: **You unlocked Wizard mode**, what
earned it, and **Switch to Wizard** or **Later**. It waits for a quiet moment,
never opening over another dialog or a form with unsaved changes, and it shows
once, whichever browser you use: the panel keeps that it was shown. Later
leaves everything as it is; the mode waits on the Modes page with a **New**
badge.

What the panel counts for this stays in its own database and is sent
nowhere, and nothing you type in Ctrl+K is kept.

## Effects

Each mode keeps its own switches:

- **Pointer**: a light that follows your cursor (the cursor itself stays as
  it is), star dust behind it, and a few stars when you click a button or a
  link. With the cursor off the page it drifts around on its own, and flies
  back when the cursor returns. It hides over text fields and does nothing on
  a touch screen.
- **Elements**: what happens on the page itself, like a glow on a broken
  site's card, a soft pulse on a healthy site's dot and turning rune circles.
- **Background**: a soft glow of violet and teal at the edges of the page,
  and a few small glowing motes drifting up.

When your system asks for reduced motion, every effect stays off whatever the
switches say, and the Modes page says so. The effects draw on one canvas that
never takes a click, rest when nothing moves, and stop while the tab is
hidden.

## Wizard

Starlit colors (moon-white paper, indigo ink, violet and aurora teal in the
light theme; deep night blue in the dark one), cards as sheets of paper with
uneven edges, and everything else in a frame in the same hand: tiles, the
boxes inside cards and the New site wizard's choices with an inked edge,
fields and dropdowns on toned paper with a gold line under them, checkboxes
as small gilded boxes, the main buttons, switches, progress bars and whatever
is chosen (a filter, a view, the open section, the step you are on) in a soft
aurora from pale violet into pale teal, the open tab underlined
in gold, faint indigo scrollbars, and menus and modals as sheets with a gold
frame. Rune circles turn where something runs, and a few pages have names of
their own:

words inside pages stay plain, and so do errors, warnings, status labels
(Healthy, Needs attention, Broken) and anything that removes something.

## Circuit

A holographic control surface: glass panels with two cut corners and bracket
marks on the other two, a thin lit outline, clean glass with thin hex outlines
fading out from one corner (a few cells softly lit), square controls, and a
circuit board behind the pages. Tinted boxes on the page (notices and the
like) are mostly solid, so the board shows through them only a little.
Whatever is chosen gets a soft cyan fill and a lit bar; titles and the menu
are in a narrow technical face, spaced out. The board is part of the look and
stays when every effect is off. The effects add light running along its
traces (Background); a reticle on the cursor's tip that locks its brackets
onto the button, link or small control you point at, with rings when you
click (Pointer); and a slow glint of light over one card now and then, a
pulsing red outline on a broken site and a breathing light on a healthy one
(Elements). A segmented ring turns where something runs. Pages it names its

## Trying it out

[Developer options](pages.md#developer-options) has a Modes card. It lists
each mode and whether it is unlocked, and keeps the rest to itself: how each
mode opens, its progress and its word stay hidden behind a small puzzle you
have to find first. Solved, the card shows them, with buttons to unlock and
seal each mode and to fill or reset its paths; an unlock from there shows the
unlock dialog, as any other. The dialog opens with its Switch button ready, so
Enter switches. Switching Developer options off locks the card again.

The public demo is a showcase: every mode is open there from
the start, to try them all. How a mode opens, its word and the line for today
belong to the panel itself, so none of them ship with the demo.
